FIELD NOTE / 01

Understanding the December Shopper

Picture your guest: shopping bags in both hands, checking their phone, mentally tallying their remaining tasks. The liquor store wasn't first on the list—it was last.

They've already navigated the mall parking lot, fought the crowds at Quality Foods, wrapped gifts are in their car. Now they need wine for the office party, a bottle for their host, something for the unexpected plus-one at Christmas dinner.

Last

For a busy holiday guest, this may be the last errand before heading home.

They don't want to be here. But they need to be here. And they want it to be easy.

FIELD NOTE / 02

The Three Types of Gift Buyers

Every December shopper is buying gifts for people in one of three categories:

🤷
The Acquaintance

Coworker, neighbor, someone they don't know well

🎯
The Enthusiast

The whiskey lover, the wine collector, someone with strong preferences

😰
The Surprise

Unexpected plus-one, last-minute invitation, hostess gift

Each one needs something different. Each one is counting on you to make it simple.

💡 Your Edge

For The Enthusiast, have go-to bottles picked out. A solid bourbon at $45, a single malt Scotch at $75, a craft gin at $50. Know them cold. When someone says "my dad loves whiskey," you should have something in mind.

FIELD NOTE / 04

Presentation Is Part of the Gift

For a gift, presentation can add appeal. Start with the recipient’s taste and the guest’s spending range.

An embossed label or heavier bottle tells you about the packaging, not necessarily the wine inside.

Guest: "These two Cabernets are the same price. Which one?"
You: "Let’s compare their styles first. If both fit, this one may give you the presentation you’re looking for."

Use verified product notes and your knowledge of the wine to explain the differences.

Make the reason for your recommendation clear.

FIELD NOTE / 06

Always Ask the Budget

This is the most important question you'll ask all December:

"How much are you looking to spend?"

Not "What's your budget?" (too formal). Not "How much can you afford?" (too presumptuous). Just: How much are you looking to spend?

Why This Matters

Knowing the spending range helps you offer relevant choices. Explain what makes each option suitable, and respect the amount the guest wants to spend.

Guest: "I need a good red wine for a dinner party."
You: "Sure! How much are you looking to spend?"
Guest: "Maybe around $25-30?"
You: "Perfect. Let me show you a couple of options that would be great for that."

Now you're not guessing. You're not over-reaching or under-delivering. You're working within their comfort zone and maximizing value within that range.

💡 The Range Response

When they give you a range, show suitable options within it and explain the differences. Ask before suggesting anything above their stated amount.

FIELD NOTE / 07

Check the Style, Not the Shape

Bottle shape does not reliably tell you whether a wine is dry or sweet. Check the product notes or ask a knowledgeable colleague.

You: “Do you have a wine you already enjoy?”

A familiar favourite gives you a useful starting point. Confirm the style before making your recommendation.

Build a reliable shortlist

Use current tasting notes to learn a few dry and sweeter options in your section. Know why each fits, and ask for help when you are unsure.

FIELD NOTE / 08

The Checkout Save

They're at the register. Transaction complete. This is where you earn bonus points:

You: "Need a gift bag for this?"
Guest: "OMG, yes. Where do I-"
You: "Right here. We've got you covered."

A useful offer can save them one more errand.

Remember: the liquor store is the last stop. They're exhausted. They don't want to hunt down a gift bag at Shopper's. They want to go home.

When you offer a gift bag, you're doing them a favour they'll remember.

💡 The Psychology

Offer a gift bag, show the options and prices, and let the guest decide before adding it.

FIELD NOTE / 09

Don't Fear the Locked Cabinet

🔐

The Premium Section

Where most employees feel intimidated—and shouldn't.

Someone is looking at the locked cabinet. Some guests are browsing; others would welcome a hand. Offer help and let them guide the conversation.

The Opening Line

You: "Need a hand with anything?"

That's it. Simple. Friendly. Non-threatening.

Outcome 1: "Yes, can you get me that bottle?" (Easy.)

Outcome 2: "What's the difference between these two?" (Now we're talking.)

Outcome 3: "I'm looking for a gift for my boss who drinks single malt..."

💡 When You Don't Know

"That's a great question—I want to make sure you get the right answer. Let me grab someone who knows this section better." Honesty builds trust. Faking it loses sales.

The locked cabinet isn't intimidating. It's opportunity. Those browsers are pre-qualified-they're already looking at premium products. Your job is just to help them choose.

FIELD NOTE / 10

Low and No ABV 🥤

Not just pop anymore.

Alcohol-free options can be thoughtful gifts and drinks for the table. Learn the choices in your store and check labels for the guest’s alcohol-content requirements.

Three Types of Shoppers

The DD: "I'm driving—what can I bring that's not just pop?"
The Sober Curious: Done with hangovers, still wants to toast.
The Thoughtful Host: "My sister doesn't drink—I want something nice for her."

They all want the same thing: something that feels special.

The Opening Line

You: "Looking for something specific in non-alcoholic, or just browsing?"

💡 The Upgrade

Ask what they enjoy and what they want to spend before suggesting another option.

What's What

Alcohol-Free Sparkling: Ask whether they enjoy a sparkling drink, then compare suitable options and check the label.

Premium Mixers: Ask which flavours they enjoy and what they plan to serve. Check ingredients and preparation directions.

Craft Sodas: Compare flavours and formats available in your store.

The Gift Play

Guest buying 2-3 premium bottles?

You: "Is this for you or are you putting together a gift?"

If they are making a gift, offer useful finishing touches within their spending range.

💡 When You Don't Know

"Great question—let me grab someone who knows this section better."

The One Question

"What's the occasion?"

That's it. Now you know what they need—and what else they might want.

FIELD NOTE / 11

You're Not Selling-You're Rescuing

Your guest arrived at your store last for a reason. Not because you're unimportant—because they're exhausted.

They've battled crowds, circled parking lots, checked off lists. They're carrying bags, mentally calculating what they've forgotten, dreading the next errand.

When you help them find the right bottle quickly—when you offer that gift bag—when you make this stop easy—you're not just making a sale.

You're sending them home.

And they'll remember that.
